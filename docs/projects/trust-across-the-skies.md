# Trust Across the Skies: Survey of All-Altitude Aero-Space Security

* Point of contact: TBD
* Suitable for: TBD
* Keywords: embedded system security, hardening and attacks, embedded inference

Today’s situation is connectivity without a shared trust floor. All-altitude
aero-space systems now couple satellites, aircraft, drones, base stations, and
cloud control planes into one operational fabric—so a fault or compromise at a
single layer can cascade upward to vehicles and platforms or downward to end
devices. The surface is no longer a single box or a single protocol: GPS
spoofing, signal jamming, avionics intrusion, and weak authentication or key
management on cross-domain links can undermine safety and mission continuity
within minutes. Pure device-level hardening cannot keep up with this stack, and
isolated case studies cannot establish whether defenses compose end to end.

Our approach treats aero-space security as a layered survey with an explicit
chain of targets, threats, and mitigations. We collect a representative map of
aero-space targets across altitudes and supporting infrastructure; study
existing attacks and defenses at hardware, protocol, and system layers; and
organize findings so cascading paths—from cloud and base station through
aircraft and satellite—are visible rather than buried in domain silos. The goal
is a reference picture that unifies trusted execution, integrity verification,
and secure orchestration without reducing the problem to sample platforms alone.

The objectives are to:

* Collect a representative list of aero-space targets (satellites, aircraft,
  drones, base stations, and supporting cloud infrastructures)
* Study existing mitigations and attacks across altitudes and cross-domain
  links (e.g., GPS spoofing, signal jamming, avionics intrusion, weak
  authentication and key management)
